# Loop Coherence Model

A system is a bounded, recurring process defined by stable transformation rules.

A sustaining loop is coherent when:
- its inputs are intrinsic to its operation,
- its continuity does not depend on non-native compensation,
- it maintains function under volume, duration, disturbance, and translation pressure,
- it does not destabilize the substrate required for its execution beyond recovery,
- it preserves its invariant under transformation.

---

## Definitions

**Intrinsic inputs**  
Required resources or conditions expected by the system’s design, not ad hoc interventions.

**Non-native compensation**  
Any external patch, intervention, or rescue required to prevent loop failure that is not part of the system’s native operation.

**Pressure classes**
- **Volume**: increased input or load  
- **Duration**: sustained operation over time  
- **Disturbance**: interruption or disruption  
- **Translation**: transfer across contexts or mediums  

**Invariant**  
The core constraint structure that defines the system’s identity, independent of its expression.

---

## Failure Modes

**Collapse**  
The loop cannot continue.

**Drift**  
The loop continues but no longer preserves its invariant.

**Dependency creep**  
The loop persists only through accumulating compensations.

**False closure**  
The loop appears stable in a narrow domain but fails under pressure or translation.

---

## Operational Check

1. What is the loop?  
2. What inputs does it require?  
3. Are those inputs intrinsic or compensatory?  
4. Which pressure class breaks it first?  
5. Does it drift under sustained operation?  
6. Does it destabilize its substrate?  
7. Does it survive translation without reinterpretation?  
8. If it persists, does it remain aligned with its invariant?  

---

## Scope

This model evaluates persistence and failure modes.  
It does not determine desirability or worth.
